Habits that catch most misses

Write the type first (e.g. "L5 photoelectric"), then the formula, then numbers.

Per one vs per mole: hν and hc/λ give J for one photon or one atom. Tables give kJ/mol. Convert before comparing.

Diatomic: H₂ N₂ O₂ F₂ Cl₂ Br₂ I₂. O₂ = 32.00.

Keep every digit until the end; round once. Molar masses to 2 decimals.

Direction check: shorter λ = more energy. Squeezing a gas = smaller V. Answer in the unit asked (nm, kJ/mol, g).

Gases

G1One set of conditions

one P, one V, one T; asks P, V, n, grams, or # of atoms

PV = nRT
  1. List P, V, n, T. Convert: atm, L, mol (g ÷ M), K.
  2. Rearrange for the unknown with letters (n = PV/RT, V = nRT/P…).
  3. Plug in. Grams: × M. Atoms/molecules: × Nₐ.

Ex: 2.00 g O₂ at 25 °C, 1.00 atm. n = 2.00/32.00 = 0.0625 mol. V = (0.0625)(0.08206)(298.15)/1.00 = 1.53 L

Sets: GR 1, 2, 3, 4 · GPS 5, 7, 9

G2Before → after (same gas)

"rises to", "is heated to", a bubble moves up a lake

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
  1. List state 1 and state 2. T in K.
  2. Cross out anything that stays the same.
  3. Multiply both sides to isolate the unknown, then plug in.

Ex: 1.0 L at 1.0 atm, 300 K → 2.0 atm, 400 K. V₂ = P₁V₁T₂/(T₁P₂) = (1.0)(1.0)(400)/(300·2.0) = 0.67 L

Sets: GPS 6

G3Density / molar mass / identify the gas

"density in g/L", "molar mass", "identify the gas", empirical formula + gas data

d = PM/RT
M = grams ÷ n, n = PV/RT
  1. Density: plug P (atm), M, T (K) into d = PM/RT.
  2. Molar mass: n = PV/RT, then M = g ÷ n.
  3. Identify: match M to the periodic table (noble gas) or to SO₂/SO₃. Molecular formula: M ÷ M(empirical) = multiplier.

Ex: 0.500 g in 250 mL at 1.00 atm, 300 K. n = (1.00)(0.250)/(0.08206·300) = 0.01015 mol. M = 0.500/0.01015 = 49.2 g/mol

Sets: GPS 8, 10, 11 · GR 5, 6 (key's 31.6 is wrong; it's 121), 7

G4Partial pressure / mole fraction

several gases with moles; "partial pressure of Ar"

X_A = n_A / n_total
P_A = X_A · P_total
  1. Add all moles for n_total.
  2. X = n_A ÷ n_total.
  3. × P_total (answer keeps P_total's unit).

Ex: 1 mol A + 3 mol B at 800 mmHg. X_A = 0.25 → P_A = 200 mmHg

Sets: GPS 12

G5Flasks opened and mixed

table of flasks with P and V; valves open; "final pressure"

P_final = (P₁V₁ + P₂V₂ + P₃V₃) / V_total
  1. Multiply P × V for each flask.
  2. Add them.
  3. ÷ total volume of all opened flasks. Same P in every flask.

Ex: 2.00 atm × 1.00 L and 1.00 atm × 3.00 L. (2.00 + 3.00)/4.00 = 1.25 atm

Sets: Exam 1.5

G6Density of a mixture

"% by volume" of each gas + P and T; asks density

M_avg = X_A·M_A + X_B·M_B + …
d = P·M_avg / RT
  1. % ÷ 100 = mole fraction X.
  2. Weighted M_avg (diatomics doubled: F₂ = 38.00, N₂ = 28.02).
  3. d = PM/RT.

Ex: 50% N₂, 50% O₂ at 1 atm, 0 °C. M = 0.5(28.02) + 0.5(32.00) = 30.01. d = 30.01/(0.08206·273.15) = 1.34 g/L

Sets: GPS 14 · Exam 2.5

G7Gas stoichiometry (incl. limiting + final P)

balanced reaction + a gas; "how many L produced", "what mass", "final pressure after ignition"

given → mol → × (coef want / coef given) → mol → answer
  1. Balance the equation.
  2. Given → mol: g ÷ M, or M × L (solution), or PV/RT (gas).
  3. Two amounts given? Limiting = the one that runs out first.
  4. Mol ratio → mol wanted → V = nRT/P or g = mol × M.
  5. Final pressure: n = all gases left (product + leftover excess + H₂O(g)), P = nRT/V.

Ex: 10.0 g CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ at STP. 10.0/100.09 = 0.0999 mol CO₂ × 22.4 = 2.24 L. Density of a sealed mixture = total mass ÷ V (mass never changes).

G8Fuel burned with air

octane/hexane + "mole fraction of O₂ in air is 0.2095"

mol air = mol O₂ ÷ X(O₂)
mol O₂ = mol air × X(O₂)
  1. Balance: C₈H₁₈ + 12.5 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 9 H₂O.
  2. Fuel: mL × density → g ÷ M → mol.
  3. × 12.5 → mol O₂, ÷ X → mol air.
  4. V = nRT/P (or × 22.4 at STP). Backward problem (air given): run the chain in reverse.

Ex: 1.00 mL octane (0.70 g/mL). 0.70/114.23 = 0.006128 mol × 12.5 = 0.0766 mol O₂ ÷ 0.21 = 0.365 mol air × 22.4 = 8.18 L at STP

G9Collected over water

"collected over water / by displacing water at __ °C"

P_gas = P_atm − P_H₂O
  1. P_H₂O from table (page 4) at that T.
  2. Subtract, ÷ 760 → atm.
  3. n = P_gas·V/RT, then stoichiometry if asked (G7).

Ex: 0.500 L H₂ at 25 °C, 760 mmHg. 760 − 23.8 = 736.2 mmHg = 0.9687 atm. n = (0.9687)(0.500)/(0.08206·298.15) = 0.0198 mol

G10Reaction solved in pressures

gas A + gas B with starting pressures, constant T and V; "total pressure after"

constant T and V → P ∝ n (treat atm like mol)
  1. Find limiting reactant using pressures × coefficients.
  2. Before / change / after table in atm.
  3. Add everything left (gases only).

Ex: A + 2B → C, 2.00 atm each. B limiting: uses 2.00 B, 1.00 A, makes 1.00 C. Left: 1.00 A + 1.00 C = 2.00 atm

Sets: GPS 15 · Exam 2.2

G11Manometer

U-tube of mercury with height h

  1. Closed end (vacuum): P_gas = h.
  2. Open end, mercury higher on the open side: P_gas = P_atm + h.
  3. Open end, mercury higher on the gas side: P_gas = P_atm − h. cm × 10 = mmHg.

Why: the side with more push shoves the mercury away. Opening a stopcock to more gas pushes harder, so h grows.

Sets: GPS 3, 4 · HW7 MCQ

G12KMT concepts

"assumptions of the ideal gas law", "fastest", "highest average KE"

  1. Molecules have negligible volume.
  2. Constant random motion; elastic collisions.
  3. No attractions or repulsions.
  4. Average KE ∝ T (K): same T → same KE.

Speed: same T → lighter is faster (u = √(3RT/M)). Same T → same KE for every gas.

Ch 8 Quantum: light and the hydrogen atom

The whole chapter is one chain

λ (m) ⟷ c = λν ⟷ ν (s⁻¹) ⟷ E = hν ⟷ E one photon (J) ⟷ × Nₐ ⟷ E per mole (J/mol)

L1λ ⟷ ν

gives frequency, asks wavelength (or reverse)

c = λν → λ = c/ν, ν = c/λ
  1. λ in m. ν in s⁻¹ (= Hz).
  2. Divide c by the one you have.

Ex: 650 nm = 6.50×10⁻⁷ m. ν = 3.00×10⁸ / 6.50×10⁻⁷ = 4.62×10¹⁴ s⁻¹

Sets: QS 8

L2Photon energy / # of photons

"energy of one photon", "per mole", "how many photons in __ kJ"

E = hc/λ (one photon)
# photons = E_total ÷ E_one
  1. λ → m. E_one = hc/λ.
  2. Per mole: × Nₐ.
  3. How many: total (J, not kJ) ÷ E_one.

Ex: 400 nm. E = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(3.00×10⁸)/(4.00×10⁻⁷) = 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ J. 1.00 J holds 1.00/4.97×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.01×10¹⁸ photons

Sets: QS 9, 15

L3Bond energy → longest λ

"breaking a bond requires __ kJ/mol", "longest wavelength that can break it"

E (one bond, J) = kJ/mol × 1000 ÷ Nₐ → λ = hc/E
  1. Per mole → per one bond (× 1000 ÷ Nₐ).
  2. λ = hc/E. "Longest" because one photon must at least pay the bond energy; longer λ = weaker photon.
  3. × 10⁹ if they want nm.

Ex: 400 kJ/mol → 4.00×10⁵/6.022×10²³ = 6.64×10⁻¹⁹ J. λ = 1.988×10⁻²⁵/6.64×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.99×10⁻⁷ m = 299 nm

Sets: QS 18 · HW9 6 · Exam 1.3

L4Photoelectric effect

"threshold frequency", "binding energy", "ejected electrons", "maximum velocity"

E_photon = φ + KE
KE = ½mv², φ = hν₀
  1. Threshold only: φ = hν₀ (J per electron). Per mole: × Nₐ.
  2. Given λ and electron speed: E_photon = hc/λ; KE = ½(9.11×10⁻³¹)v².
  3. φ = E_photon − KE. × Nₐ ÷ 1000 → kJ/mol.

Ex: 300 nm photon = 6.63×10⁻¹⁹ J; electron at 8.0×10⁵ m/s has KE = ½(9.11×10⁻³¹)(8.0×10⁵)² = 2.92×10⁻¹⁹ J. φ = 3.71×10⁻¹⁹ J × Nₐ = 223 kJ/mol

Sets: QS 10, 19 · HW9 7

L5H-atom transition (n's given)

"falls from n = 7 to n = 4", "energy to excite 1 → 4", "frequency emitted"

ΔE = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸ (1/n_f² − 1/n_i²)
  1. Plug n_final and n_initial in that order.
  2. Negative = emitted (falls). Positive = absorbed (goes up). Photon energy = the size.
  3. λ = hc/|ΔE| (× 10⁹ for nm), or ν = |ΔE|/h.

Ex: 3 → 2: ΔE = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸(1/4 − 1/9) = −3.03×10⁻¹⁹ J (emitted). λ = 1.988×10⁻²⁵/3.03×10⁻¹⁹ = 656 nm

Sets: QS 11, 12, 13 · (QS 14: λ given → just E = hc/λ)

L6H-atom transition (find the starting n)

gives λ of the photon(s) + the final level; asks the original n

E_photon = 2.18×10⁻¹⁸ (1/n_f² − 1/n_i²) → 1/n_i² = 1/n_f² − E/2.18×10⁻¹⁸
  1. E = hc/λ for each photon. Two steps? Add the energies (start → ground = both jumps).
  2. Solve: 1/n_i² = 1/n_f² − E/R_H.
  3. n_i = 1/√(that). Should come out close to a whole number.

Ex: 434 nm photon, falls to n = 2. E = 4.58×10⁻¹⁹ J. 1/n² = 0.2500 − 0.2101 = 0.0399 → n² = 25.1 → n = 5

L7Light energy used as heat

laser pulses melting ice, light heating water, "% efficient"

heat needed: q = m·c·ΔT or mol × ΔH_fus
# photons = q ÷ E_one; # pulses = photons ÷ photons per pulse
  1. Heat needed in J: melting = (g ÷ 18.02) × 6010 J/mol; heating = g × 4.184 × ΔT.
  2. % efficient: light needed = q ÷ (efficiency as a decimal).
  3. E_one = hc/λ. Photons = J ÷ E_one. Pulses = photons ÷ photons per pulse (or J ÷ J per pulse).

Ex: heat 5.0 g water by 15 °C: q = 5.0(4.184)(15) = 314 J. 500 nm photon = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J → 7.9×10²⁰ photons

Sets: QS 20 · HW9 8, 10 · Exam 2.1

L8de Broglie wavelength

a moving electron, ion, or ball with mass and speed

λ = h / (m·v) m in kg, v in m/s
  1. Mass in kg: g × 10⁻³. One atom/ion: (g/mol ÷ 1000) ÷ Nₐ.
  2. Speed in m/s: km/s × 1000.
  3. Plug in.

Ex: electron at 1.0×10⁶ m/s: λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴/(9.11×10⁻³¹ × 1.0×10⁶) = 7.3×10⁻¹⁰ m

Sets: QS 16, 17 · HW8 MCQ

Ch 8 concept facts (MCQ)

  1. Brightness = number of photons. Color (λ) = energy per photon.
  2. Low → high energy: radio, microwave, IR, visible (red → violet), UV, X-ray, gamma.
  3. Falling = emits (bright lines). Going up = absorbs. Same element, same λ.
  4. You can't know an electron's exact position and speed at once (uncertainty). Orbitals are probability maps.

Ch 9 Periodic properties

P1Allowed quantum numbers / name the subshell

"which set is not possible", "n = 4, l = 1 represents a ___ subshell"

n = 1, 2, 3… l = 0 … n−1 mₗ = −l … +l mₛ = ±½
l: 0 = s, 1 = p, 2 = d, 3 = f
  1. Check each rule left to right. One break = not possible.
  2. Subshell name = n + letter for l (n = 3, l = 1 → 3p).

Ex: (3, 2, −3, +½): l = 2 allows mₗ −2…+2, so not possible. (2, 1, 0, −½) = 2p.

P2Which subshell is higher in energy

"highest energy electron", "transition consistent with an increase in energy"

1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s 5f 6d 7p
  1. Turn each set into a subshell name (P1).
  2. Later in the fill order = higher energy.
  3. "Increase in energy" = moving to a later subshell.

Ex: 4s vs 3d: 3d comes later, so 4s → 3d is an increase.

P3Configuration of an atom

"write the configuration", "which element has [Xe]6s²…", "unpaired electrons"

fill order (P2); s holds 2, p 6, d 10, f 14
  1. Noble gas core in brackets, then keep filling from the next s.
  2. Element from config: count the electrons (core + rest) = Z.
  3. Unpaired: draw the boxes; one ↑ in each box before pairing (Hund).
  4. Exceptions your professor uses: Cr = [Ar]4s¹3d⁵, Cu = [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ only.

Ex: S (16): [Ne]3s²3p⁴ → p boxes ↑↓ ↑ ↑ → 2 unpaired, paramagnetic.

P4Last electron's quantum numbers / count electrons

table: element → (n, l, mₗ, mₛ) of the last electron, or a set with "--" → how many electrons

boxes labeled mₗ = −l … +l, left to right; fill ↑ (+½) in every box first, then ↓ (−½)
  1. Write the config; the last subshell gives n and l.
  2. Draw its boxes, fill electrons in order, find which box the last one lands in and its arrow.
  3. Reverse (numbers → element): that box tells you how many electrons are in the subshell → count Z.
  4. Counting: list every real subshell (1s…7p) that fits the fixed numbers, count orbitals, × 2 (or × 1 if mₛ is fixed).

Ex: Fe [Ar]4s²3d⁶: 5 ↑ in boxes −2…+2, the 6th is ↓ in −2 → (3, 2, −2, −½). Set (2, --, --, +½): 2s + 2p = 4 orbitals × 1 = 4 electrons.

P5Ions

a charge on the symbol; "isoelectronic with", "how many 3d electrons in Fe³⁺"

electrons = Z − charge (Fe³⁺: 26 − 3 = 23)
  1. Write the neutral atom first.
  2. Anion: add electrons. Cation: remove from the highest n first, so 4s leaves before 3d.
  3. Isoelectronic = same electron count.

Ex: Fe [Ar]4s²3d⁶ → Fe²⁺ = [Ar]3d⁶ (4s leaves first, not [Ar]4s²3d⁴).

P6Periodic trends

"arrange in order of radius / IE / EA", "if X is bigger than Y then…"

radius: ↓ across a row, ↑ down a group
IE and |EA|: ↑ across, ↓ down (the opposite of radius)
  1. Different rows? The lower row (bigger n) is bigger.
  2. Same row? More protons pulls harder → smaller.
  3. Isoelectronic ions: more protons = smaller (K⁺ < Cl⁻ < S²⁻).
  4. Bigger atom → lower IE, more metallic, smaller Zeff.

Ex: Na, Mg, K by radius: Mg < Na (same row, Mg has more protons) < K (next row down).

P7Successive ionization energies

list or graph of I₁, I₂, I₃…; "which element"

count the IEs before the huge jump = valence electrons = group
  1. Find the big jump (several times bigger).
  2. Number before it = group (1, 2, 13 → 3, 14 → 4…).
  3. Pick the answer in that group. I₂ of a group 1 metal is huge (core).

Ex: 738, 1451, 7733 kJ/mol: jump after 2 → group 2 → Mg.

P8ΔH from ionization energies and electron affinities

a reaction of bare gas atoms/ions (F⁻, C⁴⁺…); "heat of reaction", "g needed to produce __ kJ"

remove an e⁻ from X → +IE add an e⁻ to X → EA (table sign)
reverse a step → flip its sign
  1. For each species, list the electron steps (X⁻ → X is −EA; X → X⁺ is +I₁; X⁺ → X²⁺ is +I₂; X⁴⁺ → X³⁺ is −I₄…).
  2. Add the steps; multiply by the coefficient.
  3. ΔH (kJ per reaction as written). Grams: mol reactions = kJ wanted ÷ ΔH → × coefficient → × M.

Ex: Na(g) + Cl(g) → Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g): ΔH = I₁(Na) + EA(Cl) = 496 + (−349) = +147 kJ. Values from table pages 4–5.
